## SECTION A — The Geometry Problem

**Heading:** You don't sleep in one position. So why does your pillow have one height?

Lie on your back. The back of your skull sticks out further than your neck does — so your head needs somewhere to drop into while your neck stays supported underneath. **Low head. Supported neck.**

Now roll onto your side. Your entire shoulder width is underneath you. The gap from your ear to the mattress just got several inches taller. To keep your head level with your spine, you need substantially *more* height.

One height cannot serve both. That's not a quality issue — it's geometry. A pillow tuned for back sleeping lets your head sag sideways when you turn. A pillow tuned for side sleeping pushes your chin toward your chest when you turn back.

**And you roll over all night.** Which is why the pillow that felt perfect in the store is wrong for half of every night.

## SECTION B — What's Actually Happening at 6:00 AM

**Heading:** The pinched cable

Think of your neck as a heavy-duty power cable running up into a junction box at the base of your skull. It's built to run along a gentle curve — not straight, not folded.

Kink it, the way a garden hose folds when you drag it around a corner, and pressure builds at the fold.

That's what an unsupportive pillow does to your neck for eight hours a night. The top segments — C1 through C3 — compress. The small suboccipital muscles at the base of your skull clamp down and stay clamped.

**And here's why you feel it in your face:** the nerves from the top of your neck feed into the same relay in your brainstem that handles sensation for your forehead, temples, and eyes. One shared fuse box, two rooms. Trip it from the neck, and the alarm sounds in the face.

That's why head-directed medication underperforms and head imaging comes back clean. The fault is at the neck. It's just being reported at the wrong address.

## SECTION D — The Material

**Heading:** High-density open-cell viscoelastic foam

**Dense enough to hold its shape.** The most common complaint about contour pillows is that the middle collapses after a few weeks and you're back where you started. Density is what determines whether the geometry above still exists in month six.

**Open-celled so heat moves through it.** Closed-cell foam traps heat under your head. Open-cell structure lets it pass.
