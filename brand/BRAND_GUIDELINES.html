§01

Foundation

What the brand is arguing, to whom, and against what — everything downstream derives from this.

Positioning

For high-performing knowledge workers whose careers demand unbroken screen time but whose bodies are breaking down from it, LullyRest is the only pillow engineered around the actual mechanical cause of morning cervicogenic headaches — dual-plane sleep positioning — not another "plush comfort" product from the same bedding industry that caused the problem.

The Big Idea

The Nocturnal Lordotic Kink. Daytime fixes fail because eight hours a night on a flat, non-zoned pillow re-kinks the neck and undoes them. Relief has to happen overnight, structurally, or it doesn't happen. Working metaphor: the neck as a power cable; a bad pillow kinks it like a garden hose; the pinch trips pain "alarms" in the face.

Antagonist

Not the customer's body, not aging — the mass bedding industry, which optimized pillows for flat-packing and shipping economics instead of spinal anatomy.

Audience

32–44 core, 68% female, $100k+ household income, tech/corporate/healthcare knowledge workers. Mechanical problem-solvers: skeptical, analytical, already burned by 8–15 failed pillows. They want biomechanical rationale and risk-reversal, not adjectives.

Do

  • Default to Graphite + Paper White; add Proof Cyan only where something needs to be acted on or trusted
  • Let Line Grey divide sections — thin rules, never fills
  • Use Proof Cyan for exactly one job per screen (the CTA, or the proof stat — not both competing)

Don't

  • Set body copy in Proof Cyan on Graphite — fails AA at 3.1:1
  • Introduce a second accent color, ever — the restraint is the point
  • Use Line Grey as a text color at any size

Do

  • Set every number that's a claim — nights, zones, percentages — in Space Mono with tabular figures
  • Use uppercase H2 sparingly, as a section marker, not a paragraph style
  • Keep body measure near 60–65 characters

Don't

  • Introduce a second display face for "warmth" — the restraint is the credibility signal
  • Set long-form body copy in Space Mono — it's a data face, not a reading face
  • Italicize for emphasis — use weight (700) instead

§05

Imagery & Iconography

The recurring visual proof is mechanism, not mood — anatomy diagrams over lifestyle scenes.

The kink motif

The neck as a power cable, kinked. Graphite stroke, 3px, sharp caps — the compression point and its referred "alarms" are the only place Proof Cyan appears. This is the single recurring diagram across ads, product pages, and packaging; it should never be redrawn decoratively.

Do

  • Product macro shots on Paper White or Graphite seamless — zones and stitching legible
  • Single-source, directional lighting, like a lab or product-catalog photo
  • Anatomical/mechanism diagrams as the primary illustration system, stroke-based, 2px at 24px icon size
  • Sharp corners on frames and crops — no rounding

Don't

  • Warm golden-hour bedroom lifestyle shots — the DTC-bedding cliché this brand is positioned against
  • Soft-focus, filtered, or heavily color-graded photography
  • 3D renders, gradients, or filled illustration for the mechanism diagrams
  • Stock photos of people mid-yawn or hugging a pillow
§06

Voice & Tone

One voice, held constant; tone narrows or widens by context.

Clinical

Speaks like a PT/orthopedic researcher who's seen this pattern hundreds of times.

Not clinical-cold or jargon-heavy.

Skeptic-first

Leads with mechanism and proof — anatomy, VOC transparency, trial terms.

Not adjectives like "amazing" or "revolutionary."

Validating

Mirrors the reader's exact language before explaining anything.

Not diagnosing or correcting them.

Plain-spoken

7th-grade reading level for any mechanism explanation.

Not a biology lecture.

Precise

Specific numbers, checkable claims — 60 nights, 4 zones, C1–C3.

Not vague superlatives.

Tone by context

ContextTone dial
Mechanism explanation (PDP, ads)Confident, plain-spoken, analogy-led — "power cable," not "cervical biomechanics"
Guarantee / trial termsPrecise, neutral, numbers-forward — no urgency language layered on top
Customer support / reviews responsePatient, validating — mirror before explaining
Paid social / ad headlinesDirect and specific, still mechanism-anchored — never a bare emotional hook

Use

  • "Dual-plane," "unloading," "structural," "C1–C3" — mechanism-specific language
  • Specific numbers: 60 nights, 4 zones, 7–14 night adaptation window
  • The power-cable / kinked-hose analogy to explain referred pain

Avoid

  • "Cloud-like," "hotel-quality plush" — generic comfort/luxury bedding language
  • "Amazing," "revolutionary," miracle-cure framing
  • Urgency hooks with no mechanism behind them
